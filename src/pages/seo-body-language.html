Body language training

People believe what they see over what they hear. Body language training has two halves: reading other people accurately, and controlling what your own body says before you speak.

Reading people

Forget the myths about crossed arms. Useful reading is about change: the shift in posture when a price is mentioned, the pause before an answer, the moment eye contact drops. You learn to notice baselines and departures from them, in meetings, interviews and negotiations.

Your own signals

  • Entering a room. Pace, where you stand, when you sit.
  • Stillness. Fidgeting and constant nodding read as need. Calm reads as authority.
  • Eye contact. Held with intent, released on purpose.
  • Hands and space. Visible hands and taking up appropriate space signal certainty.
  • Colour and dress. What you wear is processed emotionally before anything else, and can be chosen deliberately.

Why it rarely works from videos

You can't see yourself the way others see you. Body language changes when someone watches your real behaviour, names the pattern and has you practise the correction in real situations, then reports back. That is how the training runs.

Questions

Can body language really be learned as an adult?

Yes. It is habit, not temperament. With feedback and deliberate practice most people see a visible change within a few weeks.

Is this online?

Yes, one to one over video. Camera-on sessions are actually ideal for body language work.

Is it included in mentorship?

Body language and vocal mastery is the second of the four phases of one-to-one mentorship.
