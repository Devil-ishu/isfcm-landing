Most people end their sentences with a question mark

Not in the words. In the pitch. The voice lifts at the end, and the listener hears a request for permission. You meant a decision. They heard a suggestion.

The usual advice is to speak in a deeper voice. I tried it when I was rebuilding my own. It works for about four minutes, then the performance slips and people can tell. Worse, it makes you harder to relate to.

Tonal Authority works the other way. Instead of putting on a voice, you train a few precise habits until they're automatic, then learn to adjust them to the person in front of you.

What you train

  • Three tones, used on purpose

    Rising to invite input, neutral to state facts, falling to decide. Most people use one by accident. You learn to choose.

  • Pauses that give people time to agree

    Short at a comma, a full beat at a full stop. Silence used as a tool, not a gap you rush to fill.

  • Emphasis that lands

    Slower pace, a small drop in pitch or a touch more volume on the one word that matters.

  • Mixed tones that sound real

    Pure tones sound performed. Real authority blends them: calm with an edge, warm but final.

  • Tuning to the listener

    Reading how a person takes things in, and adjusting your delivery so your point arrives the way you meant it.

How the training runs

  1. 1

    Diagnosis

    I listen to how you speak now and find the habit that's costing you the most.

  2. 2

    Training

    One-to-one sessions, two or three a week, with lines to practise between them in real scenarios.

  3. 3

    Conditioning

    Repetition until the new voice needs no effort, then testing it in the rooms that matter to you.
